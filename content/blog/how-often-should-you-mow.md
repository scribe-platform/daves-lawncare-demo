One of the most common questions we get: "How often do you mow?" The honest answer is that it depends on the season, the grass type, and how fast your lawn is growing — not on a fixed schedule.

## The One-Third Rule

The most important principle in mowing is never remove more than one-third of the grass blade in a single cut. Removing more than that stresses the plant, reduces its ability to photosynthesize, and can turn your lawn brown even in the middle of summer.

This means the mowing frequency should follow growth, not the calendar.

## Spring and Fall: More Often

Cool-season grasses (fescue, bluegrass, ryegrass) grow fastest in spring and fall when temperatures are between 60 and 75°F. During these periods you may need to mow every five to seven days to stay within the one-third rule.

## Summer: Back Off

In the heat of summer, cool-season lawns slow down significantly. Mowing every 10 to 14 days may be plenty. If you're battling drought, consider skipping a mow entirely — a longer lawn blade shades the root zone and helps the plant survive.

Warm-season grasses (Bermuda, Zoysia, St. Augustine) are the opposite: they peak in summer and slow in cooler weather.
